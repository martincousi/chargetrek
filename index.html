ChargeTrek

Game design by Houalef et al.; this is an independent browser version. Houalef A-R, Mendoza JE, Delavernhe F, Senouci S-M (2026). ChargeTrek: A gamified visual learning framework for EV charging in V2G systems. Applied Energy 414:127853. doi:10.1016/j.apenergy.2026.127853

An electric car is plugged in at home from arrival to departure. Every 15 minutes, charge, stay idle, or discharge (sell energy back to the grid). Pay as little as possible, and leave with exactly the target state of charge (the yellow cell). Prices are synthetic.

Keys: ↑ or C charge, →, Space or I idle, ↓ or D discharge, Z undo, R restart, N / P next / previous day, O reference paths (after departure).

Grid: time runs left to right in 15-minute columns, state of charge bottom (0 %) to top (100 %). Column colour is the day-ahead forecast: green cheap, red expensive. Once a column's real-time price is known, it turns bluish if the real price was above the forecast, and fades as the forecast error grows. The outlined cells ahead are where each action would take you; red outline: the target could no longer be met.

Battery: charging adds 2 % per step below 80 % and 1 % above; discharging removes 2 % per step and never goes below 20 %. A step that would pass the target stops at it. With 80 % efficiency, 1 % stored costs 0.94 kWh from the grid and 1 % discharged sells 0.6 kWh.

Money bar (right of the grid): net cost grows upward in red, net revenue downward in green.

The game design, battery model, driver profiles (Table 1) and reward (Eqs. 3 to 6) are from Houalef et al. (2026), published open access under CC BY 4.0. This page is not the authors' release and uses none of their code or data: prices are generated by a seeded model with a daily shape, because the paper's CAISO price data are not shipped here. Assumptions and deviations are listed in README.md.
